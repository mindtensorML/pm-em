EM Infrastructure Portfolio Process

How to run an emerging-market infrastructure portfolio from mandate and policy through sourcing, approval and close, to monitoring, watchlist and exit.

v2.2, October 2026Mapped against the IFC Performance Standards (2012) and the Operating Principles for Impact Management (2019)A general method with no client data

The map is written for a portfolio team at a development finance institution, an infrastructure debt fund or a blended finance vehicle. It runs in 8 steps from the mandate to the watchlist and back. Each task cites the public standard that speaks to it. 38 of 77 tasks and gates rest on practitioner practice because no standard covers them, and they are marked as such. The first version of this map was turned into a skills file that a frontier model followed through most of the process. This revision keeps the same 8 steps in the same order.

Who decides

Every task and gate has a named owner role, 27 roles in all. Credit approval, restructuring and write-offs stay with named committees. Negotiations, commitments and judgments on counterparties stay with people. Where a model drafts, the owner reviews and signs. The tags are design estimates of what a frontier model could do with the owner accountable. They are not measured results.

Step 01

Mandate and Governance

What good looks likeThe principal, its beneficiaries and its capital objectives are written down before any deal is looked at. Every later decision has a named committee and a delegation limit.
Sources · OPIM Principles 1, 2; IFC SP 2012 para 21; BCBS ECL Principle 1; Practitioner practice
Output · Mandate statement
  1. 1.1HybridPortfolio head

    Identify the principal and its constitutive limits

    A DFI, a fund sponsor or a blended finance vehicle. Record what its charter, shareholders or limited partners allow and forbid. A model can extract this from the founding documents. The portfolio head confirms it.

    Practitioner practice
  2. 1.2HybridPortfolio head

    Define beneficiaries and the development mandate

    Who the portfolio is meant to benefit and which development goals it serves. The impact objectives should be consistent with the investment strategy and proportionate to the portfolio's size.

    OPIM Principle 1
  3. 1.3HybridChief investment officer

    Set capital objectives

    Return target, impact overlay and the priority given to capital preservation. Say how impact and return are traded off at portfolio level.

    OPIM Principles 1, 2; Practitioner practice
  4. 1.4Human-onlyBoard of directors

    Establish governance and delegation

    Investment committee membership, a delegation matrix and approval thresholds. Name the committees that approve credit, restructurings and write-offs, and the point above which the board decides. Risk, compliance and E&S keep an independent voice. These are judgments about people and authority, so they stay human.

    IFC SP 2012 para 21; BCBS ECL Principle 1; Practitioner practice
  5. 1.5HybridChief investment officer

    Define portfolio architecture

    Direct lending, equity, guarantees and fund investments as separate sleeves, with target weights. Say where any donor or concessional money sits and how it is kept apart from own-account capital.

    Practitioner practice
  6. 1.6HybridPortfolio head

    Formalize the mandate statement

    One document that brings 1.1 to 1.5 together. A model drafts it from the agreed inputs. The portfolio head signs it for board review in Step 2.

    Practitioner practice

Step 02

Investment Policy

What good looks likeReturn, risk appetite, limits, E&S, integrity and impact rules, valuation basis and constraints sit in one policy. The board approves it before sourcing starts, and every later gate tests against it.
Sources · BCBS LEX paras 14, 16, 19-20, 61; IFC PS 2012; IFC SP 2012 paras 19, 22, 40; IFC EL; EDFI EL; FATF R.10; OPIM Principles 1, 2, 5; MDB PA s1.1; IPEV 2025 s1.1, s2.1; BCBS ECL Principles 1-3
Output · Investment policy statement, approved by the board
  1. 2.1HybridChief investment officer

    Return and performance objective

    A floating-rate spread over a reference rate such as SOFR, a real return floor, or both. State it net of expected credit losses and costs.

    Practitioner practice
  2. 2.2HybridChief risk officer

    Risk appetite and portfolio limits

    Credit loss tolerance and limits by single name, connected group, sponsor, country, sector and currency. The Basel large exposure rule caps one counterparty or connected group at 25 percent of Tier 1 capital. It exempts sovereigns and sets no country or sector limit, so those come from the principal's own risk policy. Multilateral lenders are not bound by Basel and use it as a reference. Say who may approve an exception to an internal limit. A breach of a regulatory limit cannot be approved away.

    BCBS LEX paras 14, 16, 19-20, 61; Practitioner practice
  3. 2.3HybridHead of E&S and impact

    E&S, integrity and impact framework

    Adopt the IFC Performance Standards and an exclusion list. Set the E&S categories, the integrity and know-your-customer standard, impact indicators, the additionality test and the Paris alignment rule. Name the indicator set, for example HIPSO or IRIS. Compliance sets the integrity standard within it.

    IFC SP 2012 paras 19, 22, 40; IFC PS 2012; IFC EL; EDFI EL; OPIM Principles 1, 2, 5; MDB PA s1.1; FATF R.10
  4. 2.4HybridChief investment officer

    Time horizon

    Fund life or balance-sheet horizon, deployment period and exit window. Infrastructure debt often runs fifteen years or more, so match the horizon to tenor.

    Practitioner practice
  5. 2.5HybridLegal counsel

    Constraints and structural advantages

    Regulatory limits, co-lending with other DFIs, and preferred creditor treatment where the principal has it. Most multilateral lenders receive that treatment in practice. Most bilateral DFIs and private funds do not, and the policy should not assume it.

    Practitioner practice
  6. 2.6HybridChief investment officer

    Select the benchmark

    An absolute return target or a reference index. Say how impact results are reported beside it.

    Practitioner practice
  7. 2.7HybridChief financial officer

    Set valuation, provisioning and reporting basis

    Loans at amortized cost with expected credit loss provisions. Equity and fund stakes at fair value under the IPEV guidelines. Fix the measurement dates, the valuation committee and the provisioning approver.

    IPEV 2025 s1.1, s2.1; BCBS ECL Principles 1-3; Practitioner practice
  8. 2.8HybridPortfolio head

    Formalize the investment policy

    A model drafts the policy statement from 2.1 to 2.7 and checks it against the mandate. The portfolio head signs it for the board.

    Practitioner practice
  9. 2.9Decision gateBoard of directors

    Board approves mandate and policy?

    The board approves, or sends back the part it objects to. A revision re-enters Step 1 at that element, the principal's limits, the beneficiaries and mandate, the capital objectives or the governance, and every later element is redone from there.

    Practitioner practice

Step 03

Opportunity Set and Sourcing

What good looks likeThe universe is narrow enough to say no quickly. Sectors are tiered against the exclusion lists and Paris alignment, and currency and blended finance rules are fixed before any deal arrives.
Sources · MDB PA lists; EDFI FF; IFC EL; DFI BCF Box 1, Annex 1; Practitioner practice
Output · Eligible universe
  1. 3.1HybridChief investment officer

    Sector allocation tiers

    Power, transport, digital, water and sanitation, and social infrastructure. Tier each against the joint MDB lists of activities that are always or never aligned with the Paris mitigation goals, and against the exclusion lists. Those lists cover mitigation only, so adaptation is still tested deal by deal.

    MDB PA lists; EDFI FF; IFC EL
  2. 3.2HybridChief investment officer

    Geography tiers

    Core emerging markets, frontier markets and fragile states, with country limits from 2.2. Fragile states need their own risk and integrity rules.

    Practitioner practice
  3. 3.3HybridChief investment officer

    Instrument menu

    Senior secured loans, mezzanine, equity, guarantees and local-currency lending. Note which instruments the principal can hold under its charter.

    Practitioner practice
  4. 3.4HybridTreasurer

    Currency exposure and hedging policy

    The share of hard-currency loans to local-currency earners that is acceptable, the hedging tools available in each market, and the ceiling on unhedged exposure. Hard-currency debt against local revenue moves the FX risk to the borrower and comes back as credit risk.

    Practitioner practice
  5. 3.5HybridBlended finance lead

    Blended finance and first-loss rules

    When concessional or first-loss money may be used, and on what terms. Apply the DFI Enhanced Principles. Use the minimum concessionality needed, crowd private money in, and avoid distorting the market. Who provides the first loss and how donor funds are governed is written down here.

    DFI BCF Box 1, Annex 1
  6. 3.6HybridHead of origination

    Sourcing channels

    DFI co-financing pipelines, sponsor relationships and government PPP pipelines. A model can track published pipelines. Relationships stay with people.

    Practitioner practice
  7. 3.7HybridChief investment officer

    Formalize the eligible universe

    A model drafts the universe document from 3.1 to 3.6. The chief investment officer signs it.

    Practitioner practice
4
Step 04

Deal Pipeline and Thesis

What good looks likeExclusions and integrity problems are found before money is spent on diligence. E&S due diligence produces an action plan that becomes a condition of investment. The rating is set independently of the deal team before the memo is final.
Sources · IFC EL; EDFI EL; EDFI FF; IFC SP 2012 paras 19, 22, 28, 30, 40; IFC PS1 para 16; FATF R.6, R.7, R.10, R.12, R.24; OPIM Principles 3-5; MDB PA s1.1; MDB PA lists; CF Mitigation v4; DFI BCF Box 1; EP4 Principles 1, 2, 4, 7; BCBS ECL Principles 3, 6, paras 41, 44
Output · Investment memo and risk assessment
  1. 4.1HybridInvestment officer

    Origination and initial screening

    Test each lead against the eligible universe and the limits. A model can run the first screen. The investment officer decides what to pursue and records the date the deal is mandated, which fixes the list versions used at 4.2.

    Practitioner practice
  2. 4.2Decision gateHead of E&S and impact

    Excluded activity?

    Any activity on the exclusion lists the principal applies stops the deal. Use the version of each list in force on the mandate date recorded at 4.1. Otherwise E&S sets a provisional category A, B or C, which decides the depth of E&S due diligence.

    IFC EL; EDFI EL; EDFI FF; IFC SP 2012 paras 19, 40
  3. 4.3HybridIntegrity and compliance officer

    Integrity due diligence, know-your-customer and sanctions screening

    Identify the beneficial owners. Screen sponsors, owners, directors and main contractors against sanctions lists, MDB debarment lists and politically exposed person lists, and run adverse media checks. A model runs the list matches and drafts the profile. The compliance officer judges every hit.

    FATF R.6, R.7, R.10, R.12, R.24; Practitioner practice
  4. 4.4Model-runInvestment officer

    Research aggregation

    Compile the information memorandum, technical reports, legal and regulatory material and site-visit notes into one indexed file. People make the site visits.

    Practitioner practice
  5. 4.5HybridCountry risk analyst

    Political, sovereign and convertibility risk

    Expropriation, transfer and convertibility, political violence and breach of contract by the state. Include mitigants such as political risk insurance or an MDB umbrella.

    Practitioner practice
  6. 4.6HybridInvestment officer

    Preliminary due diligence

    Commercial, technical, legal and permitting checks deep enough to decide whether full diligence is worth paying for.

    Practitioner practice
  7. 4.7HybridDevelopment impact lead

    Additionality and expected impact

    Why this capital is needed and what it adds beyond the market. Who benefits, by how much, and how likely that is. Use the indicators fixed in 2.3.

    OPIM Principles 3, 4; DFI BCF Box 1
  8. 4.8Decision gateInvestment committee

    Proceed to detailed due diligence?

    Concept review. Integrity must be cleared by compliance before the deal can proceed. An unresolved integrity red flag kills the deal. Open questions send the team back to preliminary due diligence.

    Practitioner practice
  9. 4.9HybridInvestment officer

    Detailed due diligence and financial modeling

    Construction risk, offtake, FX structure, the sponsor's corporate governance, and the lenders' technical, legal and insurance advisers. A model can build and check the financial model. The investment officer owns the base case.

    Practitioner practice
  10. 4.10HybridE&S specialist

    E&S due diligence and action plan

    Review against the Performance Standards. People make the site visit and lead consultation with affected communities. A model drafts the gap analysis, and the gaps become an Environmental and Social Action Plan that is a condition of investment. Where the Equator Principles apply, every Category A project, and Category B projects where appropriate, get an independent review by an independent E&S consultant. For a Category B project, due diligence by a multilateral or bilateral DFI or an export credit agency may be taken into account in deciding whether that review is needed. Where they do not apply, the principal's own policy sets when an independent review is needed.

    IFC SP 2012 paras 28, 30; IFC PS1 para 16; EP4 Principles 2, 4, 7
  11. 4.11HybridClimate finance specialist

    Paris alignment and climate finance tagging

    Test alignment with both mitigation and adaptation goals. Tag mitigation finance against the joint MDB and IDFC list of eligible activities. Adaptation finance has its own joint method. The alignment result stays an expert judgment.

    MDB PA s1.1; MDB PA lists; CF Mitigation v4; EP4 Principle 2
  12. 4.16Decision gateHead of E&S and impact

    Paris aligned?

    Applies the Paris alignment rule set in 2.3. Under the joint MDB method a deal must be aligned on both mitigation and adaptation. A deal that fails can come back restructured, for example with resilience measures for physical climate risk, or it is declined. Where the principal has made no Paris commitment, the gate records that and the deal moves on.

    MDB PA s1.1
  13. 4.12Decision gateHead of E&S and impact

    Meets the Performance Standards in reasonable time?

    Yes with an action plan, restructure and resubmit, or decline.

    IFC SP 2012 paras 22, 28
  14. 4.13HybridInvestment officer

    Structuring, FX hedging and blended finance

    Instrument, tenor, security, currency and hedge, for example local-currency lending or a cross-currency swap. Where concessional or first-loss money is proposed, show it meets the rules in 3.5 at minimum concessionality.

    DFI BCF Box 1; Practitioner practice
  15. 4.14HybridCredit risk officer

    Valuation, pricing and internal risk rating

    Credit spread, probability of default, loss given default and risk grade, plus an impact score. The deal team may propose the grade. Credit risk reviews it independently and sets the final rating.

    BCBS ECL Principles 3, 6, paras 41, 44
  16. 4.15HybridInvestment officer

    Investment memo and scenario analysis

    Base, upside and downside cases, kill criteria, the rating, the action plan, the integrity findings and the expected impact. A model drafts. The investment officer signs.

    OPIM Principle 4; Practitioner practice
5
Step 05

Decision, Approval and Portfolio Construction

What good looks likeThe committee sees limit headroom, stress results and an independent risk opinion before it decides, not after. Any limit exception is approved explicitly. Approvals above delegated authority go to the board.
Sources · BCBS LEX paras 16, 18-20; BCBS ECL para 41; IFC SP 2012 paras 21, 40; FATF R.10; Practitioner practice
Output · Approved position and audit trail
  1. 5.1Model-runChief risk officer

    Portfolio fit and limit headroom

    Size the position and compute headroom against name, connected group, sponsor, country, sector and currency limits. Flag any breach as an exception for the committee. The chief risk officer is accountable for the numbers.

    BCBS LEX paras 16, 19-20; Practitioner practice
  2. 5.2Model-runChief risk officer

    Stress test

    FX shock, political event, construction delay and refinancing risk, on scenarios that risk sets. A model runs them at deal and portfolio level.

    Practitioner practice
  3. 5.3HybridCredit risk officer

    Independent risk opinion

    Credit risk's own view of the deal, the rating and the exceptions. A model can draft from 5.1 and 5.2. Credit risk signs it.

    BCBS ECL para 41; Practitioner practice
  4. 5.4HybridInvestment officer

    Committee package and compliance clearance

    Memo, risk opinion, E&S action plan, impact case and an integrity clearance refreshed to the meeting date. The compliance officer confirms the clearance. Where the principal discloses projects before approval, the E&S summary is published for the period its disclosure policy sets for the category.

    IFC SP 2012 paras 21, 40; FATF R.10
  5. 5.5Decision gateInvestment committee

    Investment committee decision

    Approve, approve with conditions, revise and resubmit, or decline. Conditions carry into the conditions precedent in Step 7.

    Practitioner practice
  6. 5.8Decision gateInvestment committee secretariat

    Within delegated authority?

    The committee secretariat keeps the delegation matrix from 1.4 on the committee's behalf and checks each approval against it, for example on size, E&S category or a policy exception. Legal counsel rules on a case the matrix does not settle. A deal within the committee's authority moves on. A deal above it goes to the board.

    Practitioner practice
  7. 5.6Decision gateBoard of directors

    Board approval above delegation

    The board decides only on deals above the committee's delegated authority.

    IFC SP 2012 para 21; Practitioner practice
  8. 5.7HybridPortfolio head

    Finalize the target portfolio and approval record

    Record the approved terms, conditions and any limit exceptions. This record is the test for any later change in Step 7.

    Practitioner practice

Step 06

Capital Deployment Pacing

What good looks likeCommitments, disbursements, repayments and hedges are forecast together, so the portfolio never commits money it cannot fund in the currency it needs.
Sources · Practitioner practice
Output · Pacing plan
  1. 6.1Model-runPortfolio head

    Commitment schedule by vintage

    Add the approved position to the commitment and disbursement forecast by year. A model keeps the schedule. The portfolio head is accountable.

    Practitioner practice
  2. 6.2Model-runPortfolio head

    Net cash flow projection

    The J-curve for a fund, or disbursements less repayments for a balance sheet. Staged construction drawdowns go in here.

    Practitioner practice
  3. 6.3HybridTreasurer

    Liquidity buffer sizing

    Cover undrawn commitments, hedge margin calls and a stress case. A model proposes the size. Treasury signs it.

    Practitioner practice
  4. 6.4HybridTreasurer

    Funding and hedging plan

    How each commitment is funded and hedged in its currency, within the policy in 3.4. Executing a hedge is a separate human step at close.

    Practitioner practice
  5. 6.5HybridPortfolio head

    Apply the recycling rule

    Reinvest repayments or return capital, as the mandate allows.

    Practitioner practice

Step 07

Execution and Financial Close

What good looks likeThe signed documents match the approval, carry the E&S, integrity and sanctions covenants, and nothing is disbursed until every condition is evidenced and the counterparties are screened again.
Sources · IFC SP 2012 para 24; EP4 Principle 8; FATF R.6, R.7, R.10; Practitioner practice
Output · Live position
  1. 7.1Human-onlyInvestment officer

    Negotiation and documentation

    Term sheet, loan agreement and security package. Include the action plan, E&S reporting, supervision rights, integrity and sanctions representations, and remedies for breach. Negotiation and every commitment stay with people.

    IFC SP 2012 para 24; EP4 Principle 8
  2. 7.2Decision gateCredit risk officer

    Final terms within the approval?

    Credit risk compares the final terms with the approval record from 5.7. A material change goes back to the committee.

    Practitioner practice
  3. 7.3HybridLegal counsel

    Conditions precedent and regulatory approvals

    A model keeps the checklist and matches each piece of evidence to its condition. Counsel signs off each one.

    Practitioner practice
  4. 7.4HybridIntegrity and compliance officer

    Pre-disbursement integrity and sanctions refresh

    Screen the counterparties and the payment route again just before money moves. The compliance officer clears it.

    FATF R.6, R.7, R.10
  5. 7.5Decision gatePortfolio head

    Conditions precedent satisfied?

    Counsel confirms the evidence for each condition, and a clean integrity refresh from 7.4 is one of them. The outcome is satisfied, request a waiver, renegotiate, or terminate.

    Practitioner practice
  6. 7.8Decision gateInvestment committee

    Waive the condition?

    The investment committee rules on any material condition and any condition it set at 5.5. For a minor condition the 1.4 matrix may name a delegate, who acts only with credit risk's agreement. An E&S or integrity condition also needs sign-off from E&S or compliance. A waiver can defer the condition until after close or add new conditions, and it is added to the approval record from 5.7. A sanctions hit cannot be waived.

    FATF R.6; Practitioner practice
  7. 7.6Human-onlyTreasurer

    Financial close and first disbursement

    Authorized signatories sign, treasury executes any hedge, and the first money is released. Disbursement may be staged to construction milestones. Each later drawdown repeats its conditions and the integrity refresh.

    Practitioner practice
  8. 7.7HybridLoan administration

    Booking, custodian and trustee setup

    A model checks the booking against the signed agreement. Loan administration signs it off.

    Practitioner practice

Step 08

Monitoring, Reporting and Restructuring

What good looks likeEarly warning indicators move a position to the watchlist before it is past due. Restructurings and write-offs are decided by a named committee. Exits are planned with impact in mind, and lessons go back into policy.
Sources · IFC SP 2012 paras 24, 45; IFC PS1 paras 22-24, 35; EP4 Principles 8, 9; BCBS ECL A4, A13-A25, A52-A54; BCBS NPE paras 31, 36-40, 42, 44; BCBS LEX para 18; IPEV 2025 s2.1, s2.6, s2.7, s3.8, s5.16, s5.17; OPIM Principles 5-9; CF Mitigation v4; ILPA 2025
Output · Performance and impact report
  1. 8.1Model-runPortfolio manager

    Track covenants and KPIs against the thesis

    Financial covenants, construction progress and offtake volumes from borrower reports. Test each covenant on the exact definitions in the signed loan agreement, with its step-downs, and show how the borrower's reported cash flow reconciles to the defined ratio. Project headroom forward to each test date on the base and downside cases, so a breach is seen before it happens. A model reads the reports, computes the tests and flags breaches. The portfolio manager is accountable.

    EP4 Principle 8; Practitioner practice
  2. 8.2HybridE&S specialist

    Monitor the E&S action plan

    Review the annual monitoring report and progress on the action plan, with site visits by people. Where the Equator Principles apply, every Category A project, and Category B projects where appropriate, have independent monitoring after financial close. An independent consultant does it, or qualified external experts the client retains verify its monitoring information. Track grievances from affected communities and any complaint to the principal's accountability mechanism. Where the client falls out of compliance, work it back in, then use remedies.

    IFC SP 2012 paras 24, 45; IFC PS1 paras 22-24, 35; EP4 Principle 9; OPIM Principle 5
  3. 8.3Model-runPortfolio manager

    Early warning indicators

    Cover ratio trends, late payments, currency moves, sponsor distress, construction slippage, adverse media and new sanctions hits. Read construction as a month-on-month trend. Compare the forecast final cost with the budget and with last month, check that funds remain to complete, and flag contingency drawn faster than progress. A slipped completion date goes into the cash flow forecast. Also flag a hedge that ends before the loan matures and a refinancing need the borrower has not covered. A model runs the rules and raises alerts. Thresholds are set by credit risk.

    BCBS ECL A19, A24-A25; Practitioner practice
  4. 8.4Model-runPortfolio manager

    Performance attribution

    Spread, FX effect and credit migration, at position and portfolio level. Before any figure is used, reconcile positions to the books and check for stale marks and exposure counted twice.

    Practitioner practice
  5. 8.5HybridCredit risk officer

    Credit review, rating migration and IFRS 9 staging

    Re-rate each position and assess a significant increase in credit risk, using 30 days past due only as a backstop. Default is presumed no later than 90 days past due, a presumption that can be rebutted. A model drafts the staging and expected credit loss. Credit risk signs, and the named committee approves provisions.

    BCBS ECL Principles 3, 6, A4, A13-A25, A52-A54
  6. 8.6HybridValuation committee

    Valuation of equity and fair-value positions

    Value at each measurement date, usually by discounted cash flows for infrastructure. Calibrate to the entry price and backtest against realized exits. A model drafts. The valuer stays accountable for anything a model produces, as the 2025 guidelines say. The valuation committee approves.

    IPEV 2025 s2.1, s2.6, s2.7, s3.8, s5.16, s5.17
  7. 8.7HybridDevelopment impact lead

    Development impact reporting

    Jobs, emissions avoided, access to services and climate finance, against the expected impact at approval. Act when a position will not reach its intended impact.

    OPIM Principle 6; CF Mitigation v4
  8. 8.8Decision gatePortfolio and watchlist committee

    Watchlist trigger?

    Triggered by early warning rules, a downgrade, a covenant breach or an unresolved E&S breach. The committee reviews the exceptions. These are every proposed move on or off the list and every flagged name the portfolio manager proposes to leave off. The portfolio manager confirms the rest each period and the committee sees that list.

    BCBS ECL A24; BCBS NPE para 39; Practitioner practice
  9. 8.9HybridPortfolio manager

    Watchlist plan and restructuring assessment

    Agree a remediation plan with the client where one is possible. Price each option, fix, restructure or exit, on its value from today and ignore sunk cost. Show the cost of doing nothing and the date a decision is needed. Assess whether any concession is forbearance. A forborne position keeps its flag until two tests are met. All payments under the revised terms have been made on time for at least one year, counted from the scheduled start of those payments, and the borrower has resolved its financial difficulty. A new concession, or a fall into non-performing status, during that year starts the year again. A model drafts the options paper. Serious cases are flagged for transfer to the special operations unit, which the credit committee confirms at 8.11.

    BCBS NPE paras 36-40, 42, 44; Practitioner practice
  10. 8.10Human-onlySpecial operations unit

    Workout and recovery

    Standstills, negotiation with sponsors and other lenders, security enforcement and recovery. These are negotiations and binding commitments. The unit takes the case over and brings it back to the credit committee at 8.11 every monitoring period until it is cured, restructured or exited.

    BCBS NPE para 39; Practitioner practice
  11. 8.11Decision gateCredit committee

    Restructure, continue or write off?

    Return the position to monitoring once cured or restructured, start or continue a workout, or approve an exit, sale or write-off. Counsel documents any restructuring before the position returns to monitoring, and a forborne position keeps its flag. Starting a workout transfers the case to the special operations unit.

    BCBS NPE paras 31, 40; Practitioner practice
  12. 8.12HybridPortfolio head

    Reassess conditions and portfolio views

    Compare expected and actual results, financial and impact. Record lessons for policy and for the next deals.

    OPIM Principle 8
  13. 8.13Decision gateInvestment committee

    Portfolio action decision

    Hold, exit a position, or loop back. For equity and fair-value stakes, compare holding, selling and refinancing on the return from today's value, with the price at which selling and holding are equal at the hurdle. Drift beyond limits goes to a rebalance on the current book. A regime shift sends the portfolio back to strategy, and a change in the principal back to the mandate.

    Practitioner practice
  14. 8.14Human-onlyInvestment officer

    Exit execution

    Sell, refinance or prepay with the effect on sustained impact considered. Self-liquidating loans that mature are not exits in this sense. A write-off approved in 8.11 is booked at 8.18, and recovery continues where it can.

    OPIM Principle 7; IPEV 2025 s2.7
  15. 8.15HybridReporting lead

    Stakeholder reporting

    Board and limited partner reports, regulatory filings and public disclosure. An impact principles signatory discloses its alignment every year and has it verified independently at regular intervals. Fund fee and expense reports follow the ILPA Reporting Template where it applies. A model drafts. The reporting lead signs.

    OPIM Principle 9; ILPA 2025; Practitioner practice
  16. 8.16HybridPortfolio head

    Rebalance plan on the current book

    Bring the book back within limits without running a new deal through Steps 5 to 7. A model recomputes headroom on the current book and drafts the options. These are slowing new commitments where a limit is full, selling down part of a position, or buying cover such as a guarantee or credit insurance. The portfolio head signs the plan. A breach of a regulatory limit is rectified, not approved.

    BCBS LEX para 18; Practitioner practice
  17. 8.17Human-onlyPortfolio head

    Execute the rebalance

    Sell-downs and cover are negotiated and signed by people within the delegation from 1.4. Proceeds and costs are booked at 8.18. A change to new commitments goes into the pacing plan from Step 6 at its next update.

    Practitioner practice
  18. 8.18HybridFinancial controller

    Book the exit, write-off and recoveries

    A model drafts the entries for sale proceeds, a sell-down, a write-off and any provision release, and checks them against the 8.11 or 8.13 approval. The financial controller signs. Recoveries after a write-off are booked as they come in.

Open questions for practitioners

Some choices in the map rest on judgment. These are the ones where an experienced DFI portfolio head can improve it most. The current split is 6 human-only, 49 hybrid and 8 model-run tasks, with 14 decision gates.

  1. Should pacing sit before sourcing as a standing plan?

    v2 keeps Step 6 after approval so the step order matches v1. Many portfolio teams run the pacing plan continuously and use it to ration the pipeline in Step 4.

  2. Who should own the watchlist trigger?

    v2.2 lets rules raise the alert, the portfolio manager confirm names that stay where they are, and the committee review the exceptions. Some institutions let credit risk move a name alone and report it afterwards.

  3. Where should first-loss and concessional pricing be decided?

    v2 leaves it with the investment committee under the blended finance rules in 3.5. A separate committee for donor funds would manage the conflict between own-account and concessional money more clearly.

  4. How often should integrity due diligence be refreshed for sponsors already in the portfolio?

    v2 refreshes at the committee date and before each disbursement. A risk-based cycle for repeat sponsors would cost less but could miss a change in ownership.
